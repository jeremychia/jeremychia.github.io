For those who haven't heard, Grab has launched a GrabPay Angbao Promotion - giving participants a bonus ranging from $0.08 to $288 for sending an Angbao of at least $1. You can check it out [here](https://www.grab.com/sg/bettercny/angbao2019/).

The question is: does a campaign like this bring tangible benefits for [Grab](https://www.linkedin.com/company/grabapp/)? To understand this, perhaps we can look towards Metcalfe's Law.

## Metcalfe's Law

Metcalfe's Law states that a network's impact is the square of the number of nodes in the network, where nodes in this context represents us - the end users. ([Source](https://www.techopedia.com/definition/29066/metcalfes-law)) While this concept was originally conceptualised by George Glider for network devices, many find that it is relevant to the Internet's substantial growth today. In substance, this is similar to the "network effect" - the greater the number of users, the larger the impact of the network. The graph illustrates that past the critical mass crossover, the value of the network surpasses the cost of maintaining the network. Companies would therefore strive to **increase the number of *nodes*** (or users) to **increase the value of its network**.

This may, perhaps, be Grab's motive for launching this campaign.

## GrabPay's Angbao Campaign in 2019

GrabPay launched in Q4 2017, joining a fragmented e-Payments market with other players such as DBS PayLah, Qoo10's QPay etc. ([Source](https://www.straitstimes.com/tech/ride-hailing-app-grab-launches-e-payment-and-fund-transfer-system)) To thrive in this competitive environment, Grab would need to gain a substantial share of the market quickly. Grab aggressively promoted GrabPay - offering up to 10x points redemption, a strategic alliance to allow payments on [FavePay through GrabPay](https://www.straitstimes.com/business/companies-markets/grab-fave-in-strategic-tie-up-to-expand-in-singapore-and-malaysia) etc.

To internet companies like Grab, Lunar New Year is a perfect opportunity to build up brand awareness and attract new users through peer-to-peer marketing. Existing users would encourage other users to trade *Angbaos* with them, leaving both of them with minimally $0.08 in their wallets per trade. Assuming someone diligently follows with trading the maximum number of *Angbaos*, he/she would have received minimally a balance of **$10.24** (16 days * 8 people per day * $0.08). This balance can be used to make payments through GrabPay, or to book Grab rides.

Here's the catch - you'll most likely have a residual balance. You'll hardly find anything that is priced at $10.24. What happens next? While using Grabpay, you'll realise that no partial payments are allowed (i.e. you can't split your payment between Grab credits and cash/credit card). Instead, you may only opt for one payment method. Hence, you are likely to either leave the balance in your GrabPay e-Wallet, or top up additional amounts in order to use up your existing grab credits.

Unlike DBS PayLah's Angbao promotion over the 2018 Lunar New Year, this balance can't be transferred back to your bank account. This thus creates an incentive for users to top up their GrabPay credits to maximise their balance. Doing so would be supporting the original intent of Grab - to bring more users onto its GrabPay platform.

*(There is a workaround - if you have remaining balance in your GrabPay e-Wallet, you can always opt to transfer that to someone who uses GrabPay and that person can send you the same balance on other platforms (like DBS PayLah) which allows you to send that money back to your bank account.)*

Have you also noticed that GrabPay becomes your default mode of payment upon redeeming your $0.08 bonus *Angbao*? This is a subtle way of encouraging you to top-up your GrabPay credits to continue using the GrabPay platform. Pretty clever!

This begs the question - does giving out *"free"* money bring tangible benefits for Grab? With new users joining its platform, Grab is able to increase its cash holdings and vastly expand the value of the GrabPay network, so why not?

## Data is King in Industry 4.0

As a side point - the more frequently we use GrabPay for payments, the more data Grab can amass about our spending habits. This does two things:

1. It removes the data from Credit Card companies and banks we previously made payments with. (Our bank statements would simply reflect payments to "Grab", but will not contain further information on the purchases which were made)
2. Increase Grab's ability to practise targeted marketing to its consumers. (as seen in the existing GrabRewards using recommender systems)

We mustn't forget that in Industry 4.0, data is king. The more users there are on GrabPay, the more powerful its network becomes - for the data and user reach.
