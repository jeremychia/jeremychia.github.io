Many things have happened in 2018. Before I become preoccupied with what comes in the year ahead, it'd be nice to take this opportunity to jot down some thoughts on my half a year in Hong Kong.

Hong Kong is a city I have a strange affinity to - whilst I have no family in Hong Kong, it is a city that I've always felt closely connected to. It was a place I've longed for a long time to live in.

In 2018, from July to December, I got a chance to do just that.

They say you never truly know a place till you live in it. So true. I've visited Hong Kong often, but this year was the first time I found myself immersed in the local way of life - getting breakfast at the cha chaan tangs, squeezing through the crowds in the MTR, ordering food from the dai pai dongs, putting up with the insane lunch queues at Admiralty, among many others. I've had friends who entered Hong Kong as visitors lament about the bad service, cramped walkways, high prices when I tell them I love Hong Kong. I agree with them - this can be admittedly overwhelming for a newcomer. However, after getting past the initial shocks, I found that there's so much to revel in:

The *scenes* - Hong Kong is one of those cities that never sleep. In the morning, you'll see hawkers setting up their stores on the roads as the rush-hour traffic whizzes by. In the evenings you'll see the golden glow on the city's skyline - an abundance of skyscrapers backed by the mountain silhouettes. Past midnight, the city resets itself - delivery trucks line the streets; workers dragging crates to the markets, getting ready for the next day. There's always something happening at every hour - and there's a certain charm in that.

What makes it even better is that half-hour away, you'll find an escape from the city - hiking trails, vast country parks with breathtaking views.

The *language* - "under 150 years of British rule, Cantonese is accidentally preserved". In Hong Kong, Cantonese comes under the influence of English as well. For example, 多士 (do1 si6) is toast, 士多啤梨 (si6 do1 be1 lei4) is strawberry - in both cases, while the Mandarin equivalent is different, the Cantonese version resembles that of the English pronunciation. I was really glad to be able to pick up some local slang, like 𠺪 (he3) which is like *nua* in Singlish. Did plenty of that. Special thanks goes to those who shared the colloquial slang with me throughout the months - and being patient while I struggled to get it right.

The *people* - from the friends I made interning in EY over summer, the friends I made in HKU, the NTU alumni I've met, the elderly whom we worked with in Soap Cycling, to the people staying in my residence - everyone played in a part in the many happy memories which will remain etched in my mind. Thank you for sharing this entire journey with me, and I'm so grateful for the warmth and sincerity extended.

In spite of the setbacks, I still remain optimistic about my time in Hong Kong. While things have gone not-so-well, from delayed visas to taking weeks to open a bank account, Hong Kong has taught me that there's so much more to just take in and appreciate.

My gratitude goes out to my family - for supporting my endeavours, and everyone I've had the honour of meeting in Hong Kong. You've made my Hong Kong experience ever more fulfilling and I'm looking forward to seeing everyone again soon.

Hong Kong, you have my heart. I'll be back for you some day.
