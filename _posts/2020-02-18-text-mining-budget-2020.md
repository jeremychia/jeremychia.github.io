About a year ago, I penned a post on text mining Singapore's budget speeches from 2009 to 2019. There were two buzzwords that appeared frequently in the past budget speeches - "***productivity***" and "***support***".

In this piece, I will discuss observations from text mining [Singapore's Budget 2020](https://www.singaporebudget.gov.sg/budget_2020/budget-speech), and how it differs from past speeches. It appears that what we see from this speech is consistent with the government's ***support of enterprises*** and push towards ***climate change action***.

Since DPM Heng concluded his Budget 2020 speech, there were several contributors who [summarised key points](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/news/budget-sg-firms-get-covid-19-relief-4770380/). The Singapore Government's support for firms reeling from the effects of COVID-19, and other stimulus measures in place to help individuals and businesses, is pronounced in this year's budget. Some points worth noting:

1. This is Deputy Prime Minister (DPM) Heng Swee Keat's first Budget speech as a **concurrent Finance Minister and DPM**. The last two ministers to hold these positions together were: Senior Minister Tharman Shanmugaratnam and, the incumbent, Prime Minister (PM) Lee Hsien Loong. This well signals, and confirms, the message that he is set to succeed PM Lee as Singapore's next Prime Minister.
2. It is [speculated](https://www.straitstimes.com/politics/when-will-the-election-be-called-it-could-be-soon-after-budget) that the **next General Election** will be called in 2020. While the next elections must be called by April 2021, the [formation of the Electoral Boundaries Review Committee in August 2019](https://www.straitstimes.com/politics/electoral-boundaries-committee-convened-in-first-formal-step-towards-next-singapore-ge) was a clear signal of the looming elections.
3. This is Singapore's [**biggest budget deficit**](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/news/singapore/budget-2020-more-expansionary-budget-to-result-in-biggest-12446318) since 2009. The COVID-19 outbreak has a part to play - with proposed measures that were "[not on the table' a month ago](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/news/singapore/coronavirus-covid-19-budget-2020-heng-swee-keat-12442826)". There has been an notable effort to emphasise that 2020's budget deficit does not draw on past reserves.

Taking these into account, let's look at some observations from text mining:

## Surge in the use of 'Enterprises', 'Retirement' and 'Climate Change' in 2020

These words see an almost skyrocketing presence in Budget 2020. Words like '*will*', '*support*', '*provide*' and '*singaporeans*' have always been common in budget speeches. What's interesting is also, is that there are certain words ('*partnerships*', '*digital*' and '*structural*') that appear in post-2015 budget speeches, which is when DPM Heng assumed the role of Finance Minister. These are reflective of the tone and focus of DPM Heng's policies.

- '***Enterprises***': Following the successive roll-outs of [Industry Transformation Maps](https://www.mti.gov.sg/ITMs/Overview), it is no surprise that 2020's Budget speech mentions 'enterprises' twice as much for the second consecutive year. This year's measures are aimed at sustaining local employment, [developing enterprises to realise Singapore's ambition](https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/government-economy/budget-2020-enterprise-grow-package-to-help-companies-digitalise-and-enter-new) to be a Global-Asia node of technology, [weather the economic effects of COVID-19](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/news/singapore/budget-2020-s-4-billion-support-package-for-workers-firms-amid-12446082). It appears that the government is lending out support to help Singapore businesses grow, and this trend will continue under DPM Heng.
- '***Retirement***': It may be a coincidence, but the word 'retirement' spiked in 2015, an election year, too. The [Silver Support Scheme](https://www.straitstimes.com/singapore/singapore-budget-2020-more-elderly-singaporeans-to-qualify-for-silver-support-payouts), introduced in 2015, is enhanced in 2020. Measures are in place to help [those with less CPF savings to save more](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/news/singapore/budget-2020-new-scheme-to-help-those-with-less-cpf-savings-to-12446156). The minister reiterates that the government is strengthening the 'four pillars of social security' - home ownership, CPF, healthcare assurance and Workfare. This is an assuring thought for those living in Singapore - but also, be prepared to work longer.
- '***Climate Change***': Several parts of the [Resource Sustainability Act 2019](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Acts-Supp/29-2019/Published/20191004?DocDate=20191004) comes into force from 2020. Section D of the Budget 2020 speech highlights Singapore's efforts to address climate change - S$1 billion for research in Urban Solutions and Sustainability, an initial injection of S$5 billion for the Coastal and Flood Protection Fund. This follows PM Lee's National Day Rally in 2019 which warned that "[it could cost S$100 billion or more to protect Singapore against rising sea levels](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/news/singapore/ndr-2019-singapore-climate-change-costs-rising-sea-levels-11819402)". It is timely that the government is paying attention to the effects of climate change, given Singapore's position as a small island city-state. It appears likely that this will be a recurrent theme for Singapore's budgets.

## Dip in the words: "expected", "levy" and "productivity"

Indeed, we need to "expect the unexpected". While "expected" was not used in this year's budget speech, "unexpected" was featured three times (out of eight from 2009 to 2020). We are moving into uncharted waters in the age of disruption.

The [Foreign Worker Levy](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/work-permit-for-foreign-worker/foreign-worker-levy) was discussed, but as the "Foreign Worker Policy". I wonder whether the omission of the word "levy" is intentional. The general trend sees a Dependency Ratio Ceiling decrease. In 2021 to 2023, the ratio for Construction, Marine Shipyard, and Process sectors will be decreased from 20% to 15%.

I personally feel this must be carefully managed. Singapore is an open economy, and we thrive on the inclusion of best practices and ideas from all around the world. It would be beneficial for Singapore to retain its edge as a cosmopolitan, diverse city.

'***Productivity***' has seen a notable dip in usage compared to when the Productivity and Innovation Credit (PIC) was applicable. It appears that the new word that the government has opted for is "***transformation***".

## Word-Cloud and Sentiment Analysis

It appears that the use of positive and negative language in Budget 2020's speech are more pronounced compared to DPM Heng's past speeches, and even in comparison to the 2009 Financial Crisis. The front portion of DPM Heng's speech, which talks about the government's financial response to COVID-19, is negative. There are, however, attempts to use neutral and positive language in attempt to alleviate fears and boost confidence in the government.

It seems like we're poised for a rather uncertain year ahead. However, it is heartening to note the government's support for individuals and enterprises going forward amidst the virus outbreak. In addition - we will likely see climate change become a more frequented topic in Singapore's policies going forward.
